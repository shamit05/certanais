# A Place to Start

*by Matt Franey*

Our story begins in July of 2024 when my cousin suffered a life-altering stroke. Due to several circumstances, I had to step into the role of medical decision maker, legal proxy, and primary caretaker. Quickly, I was thrown into an inside view of the healthcare system I had never experienced before. I saw the good, the bad, the ugly, and the indifferent across providers and institutions, but more than anything I saw talented and caring professionals doing their utmost to help people in their most difficult moments.

At the same time, it became clear that something at a broader level was not working the way it should: continuity between providers would break down, expectations were often unclear, and communication didn’t always carry through. While I couldn’t yet connect these patterns to something specific, these experiences began to accumulate, and I started paying close attention to where things seemed to break down.

Some months later, well into this process, I was catching up on the phone with my buddy Shamit. Studying at Stanford, he was witnessing this industry from a completely different angle. Through talking, we started to piece together what Shamit was seeing in class and what I had been experiencing navigating the system. At a high level, it became clear to us that better use of technology could improve how the system functions. After a couple additional brainstorming sessions where we continued to break down the space, we identified a few avenues of exploration that could result in new products or services, applying emerging technologies to critical aspects of healthcare delivery.

Although we had not yet prioritized a specific direction, we aligned on one core principle: better information, and better access to that information, leads to better outcomes. That principle guided more discussion, and as we narrowed our focus, we zeroed in on the most immediate task in my POA juggling act: processing disability claims.

Neither of us came from this world, so, with our intellect and our passion, we approached it the only way we know how: by working with the problem directly and figuring out what held up. As we worked from this perspective, we became increasingly interested in solving this problem. We immediately immersed ourselves and created, in short order, a high-level proof-of-concept and a supporting deck. With these tools in hand, we set out to find an industry partner willing to engage with the idea in real-world cases.

Every day we gain a deeper understanding of how we can help, and our appreciation continues to grow for the work of the clinicians and professionals we now work alongside. They inspire our mission: to improve patient experiences and medical outcomes by creating technology that allows those in patient-facing roles to spend more time on care.

This mission, along with our passion for the space and sustained focus on building solutions within it, will carry us through the inevitable challenges ahead.

Our collective work matters. Lives depend on it.
